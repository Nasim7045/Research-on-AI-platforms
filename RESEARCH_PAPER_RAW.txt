# Probing Dual-Use Risks in AI Models: A Simple Framework and Safeguard Approach
## Abstract
As Large Language Models (LLMs) become standard tools for everyday work, understanding how they can be misused for illegal activities, cyber threats, and un-moderated AI art is critical. This paper evaluates how major AI models (GPT, Claude, and Gemini) react when prompted with sensitive queries regarding property law loopholes, stealth software creation, and explicit image generation. Our testing shows that platforms frequently assist with grey-area tasks—such as creating stealth session recorders, background user activity loggers, worm virus logic, and civil encroachment tactics—without verifying user intent. To address this without limiting access for daily users, we propose combining simple keyword filtering algorithms with a **Financial Friction** framework (requiring credit card or identity verification for high-risk prompts) to ensure user legal accountability.
## I. Introduction
Generative AI has made coding, legal research, and content creation accessible to everyone. However, the same helpful nature that makes AI useful for daily tasks can also be exploited by bad actors.
Standard AI safety systems try to block obviously malicious words, but users can bypass them through simple framing, hypothetical stories, or by breaking complex tasks into smaller, innocent-looking steps.
This paper focuses on three primary threat domains:
 * **Civil & Legal Law:** Seeking AI guidance to exploit legal loopholes, drag out court cases, or justify property encroachment.
 * **Cybersecurity & Stealth Tools:** Prompting AI to write silent background monitoring programs, stealth recorders, or worm software logic.
 * **Graphics & AI Art:** Utilizing fine-tuned diffusion models to generate explicit 18+ content involving celebrities, intellectual property, or minors.
## II. Real-World AI Misuse and the "Knife Rule"
When an individual uses a kitchen knife to commit a crime, society holds the individual accountable, not the manufacturer. AI models function similarly: they are dual-use tools capable of both benign and harmful applications.
If AI providers over-censor their systems to prevent all possible misuse, legitimate users lose access to essential utilities. A more effective defense allows public access for everyday tasks while introducing targeted verification steps whenever prompts touch sensitive or high-risk topics.

### D. Domain 3: Synthetic Media and Unregulated Graphics
Testing and observation of fringe image generation platforms revealed critical safety gaps in AI art synthesis:
 * **Non-Consensual & Celebrity Exploitation:** Many platforms allow users to generate explicit 18+ content featuring public figures, celebrities, and copyrighted franchise characters without consent verification.
 * **Child Exploitation Material:** Unregulated services hosting open-weight image models have been observed generating explicit art involving synthetic representations of minors, violating international criminal statutes.
 * **Lack of Metadata & Watermarks:** Most open generation platforms fail to embed immutable cryptographic watermarks (such as C2PA standards), allowing illicit media to be produced and spread anonymously.
## V. Proposed Solution: Simple Keywords and Smart Algorithms
To stop bad actors without ruining the experience for regular users, AI platforms can implement simple keyword filtering and pattern detection algorithms to screen incoming prompts.
```
[User Prompt] ──> [Keyword / Pattern Check] ──> [Is It Suspicious?]
                                                        │
                      ┌─────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┐
                      ▼                                                                   ▼
                    [NO]                                                                [YES]
                      │                                                                   │
          (Answer Prompt Normally)                                              (Trigger Financial Friction)

```
### A. Core Detection Algorithms
AI providers can deploy light, efficient algorithms to identify risky prompts instantly:
 1. **Aho-Corasick Algorithm:** A fast string-matching algorithm capable of checking a prompt against thousands of sensitive security terms (e.g., stealth, hidden background, encroach, bypass log) simultaneously.
 2. **RAKE (Rapid Automatic Keyword Extraction):** Extracts key phrases from user prompts to determine the overall intent without requiring heavy computational resources.
 3. **TF-IDF / TextRank:** Measures word importance within a query. High frequency scores for combinations like secret + record + duration mark a prompt as potential spyware.
 4. **Cosine Similarity (Vector Embeddings):** Compares user input against a database of known dangerous prompt structures. A high similarity score flags the query for safety review.
## VI. The Financial Friction Model
When a prompt is flagged as a "grey-area" request (such as generating stealth system scripts, legal evasion tactics, or sensitive graphics), the system should implement **Financial Friction**.
| User Tier | Query Type | Platform Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| **Level 1: Standard (Free)** | General questions, educational math, routine coding, general writing. | Fully open and anonymous. No extra checks required. |
| **Level 2: Grey-Area (Verification Required)** | Stealth scripts, background loggers, legal strategy, advanced utilities. | Requires valid Credit Card (CC) or identity credentials before answering. |
| **Level 3: Prohibited** | Direct malware execution, CSAM, explicit non-consensual media. | Immediate refusal, session termination, and log escalation. |
### Key Benefits of Financial Friction
 * **User Accountability:** Bad actors seeking to build malware or commit illegal acts are unlikely to attach their real credit card details or government identity to suspicious queries.
 * **Platform Liability Shift:** If a user verifies their identity and subsequently misuses the generated information for illegal purposes, the AI provider holds verifiable records to share with law enforcement. This transfers legal responsibility from the tool maker to the user.
 * **Unrestricted Benign Use:** Everyday users continue to enjoy free, friction-less AI access for normal, harmless tasks.
## VII. Conclusion
Current AI models demonstrate significant vulnerability to dual-use exploitation. As shown in empirical testing, platforms readily provide stealth recording scripts, detailed activity monitoring software, worm safety concepts, and land encroachment strategies without confirming user identity or intent.
By combining lightweight keyword detection algorithms (such as Aho-Corasick and RAKE) with a **Financial Friction** verification layer, AI developers can protect open access for the public while establishing clear legal accountability for high-risk requests.
